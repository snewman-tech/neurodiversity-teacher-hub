15-minute team activity

Staff meeting

From judgement to barrier

Choose one common statement: “won’t start”, “doesn’t listen”, “overreacts”, “is rude”, “never remembers”. As a team, rewrite it as an observable description. Then list three possible barriers and one low-risk support to trial. End by deciding how you will know whether the support helped.

30-minute discussion

Step 1

Read one learner scenario

A capable student takes 20 minutes to start independent writing, becomes defensive when prompted, and completes excellent work when an adult sits nearby.

Step 2

Identify possible barriers

Task initiation, working memory, ambiguity, perfectionism, demand sensitivity, anxiety, sensory load or fear of failure.

Step 3

Choose supports

Visible first step, modelled example, 2-minute check-back, reduced starting choices, quiet location, or collaborative planning.

Step 4

Measure impact

Did starting time reduce? Did prompting reduce? Did distress reduce? What did the student say about the change?

Staff reflection prompts

Are our supports available before crisis?

Review whether students must first fail, disclose a diagnosis or become distressed before simple supports are offered.

Do we mistake compliance for wellbeing?

Consider students who meet expectations while using unsustainable effort or masking.

Are our instructions accessible?

Audit how much information is spoken once versus kept visible throughout the task.

Do consequences teach the missing skill?

Identify whether the issue involves planning, inhibition, regulation, communication, sensory access or another skill that requires direct teaching/support.
