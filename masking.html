Masking & missed presentations

Recognise quiet, high-achieving, perfectionistic and socially camouflaging students without turning gender differences into new stereotypes.

Look for the cost of coping

Girls are among the students more likely to be missed, but masking is not exclusive to girls. Boys, gender-diverse students, high achievers, quiet students and socially motivated students may also camouflage difficulties.

Possible clues

The learner looks “fine”

  • Perfectionism or intense fear of getting it wrong
  • Copying peers to know how to behave
  • Rehearsed or scripted social responses
  • Very different presentation at home
  • Exhaustion after school
  • Strong grades hiding high effort or poor organisation
Teacher response

Support before the mask breaks

  • Ask about effort, not only achievement.
  • Offer quiet check-ins.
  • Normalise using support.
  • Reduce public performance demands.
  • Take whānau reports seriously when school presentation differs.
  • Do not require crisis-level evidence before adjustments.
Avoid replacing one stereotype with another: there is no universal “female autism” or “girl ADHD” profile. Gender, culture, personality, co-occurring conditions, environment and expectations all influence what adults notice.
