Six supports that improve access

These strategies can benefit many learners and do not require a diagnosis.

1

Make instructions visible

Pair spoken directions with short written steps, models, visuals and clear endpoints.

2

Make the start small

Replace “get on with it” with one concrete first action and a near-term check-in.

3

Build regulation into class

Movement, quiet, sensory tools and flexible seating should not require a crisis first.

4

Prepare for transitions

Warn, preview, show what comes next and identify where help is available.

5

Protect dignity

Use private correction, neutral supports and language that separates the learner from the difficulty.

6

Ask the learner

Student voice often reveals the barrier faster than adult guessing.
