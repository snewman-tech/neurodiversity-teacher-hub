ADHD can affect more than attention

At school, ADHD may affect task initiation, working memory, inhibition, time awareness, emotional regulation, planning and persistence. Performance can vary sharply with interest, novelty, urgency, fatigue and environment.

Initiation

Make starting external

Give a visible first step, model it, then check back quickly.

Working memory

Do not rely on remembering

Keep instructions, equipment lists and deadlines visible.

Time

Make time concrete

Use visual timers, interim deadlines, countdowns and planned pack-up time.

Movement

Movement can support focus

Standing, purposeful jobs, stretching or flexible seating may improve access.

Motivation

Ability ≠ easy initiation

A learner can understand the work and still struggle to activate for it.

Emotion

Regulate before reasoning

Fast, intense emotion can make language-heavy correction ineffective in the moment.

Try this instead

Instead of

“You know this. Just get on with it.”

This assumes knowledge automatically creates task initiation.

Try

“Start with the heading. I’ll come back in two minutes.”

This makes initiation smaller and gives external structure.

NZ guidance: The Ministry’s Inclusive Education ADHD guide recommends supporting focus, thinking, social interaction, positive behaviour, processing/organisation, and flexible participation and assessment.
