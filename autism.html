Design for access

Predictability

Use visual schedules and warn about changes. Explain what will happen, where, with whom, and what happens next.

Sensory access

Consider noise, lighting, crowding, touch, smells and movement before interpreting disengagement as behaviour.

Communication

Use precise language, allow processing time and check understanding without requiring eye contact.

Social demand

Structure group work and unstructured times. Offer roles, choice and recovery time.

Interests

Use focused interests as bridges to motivation, belonging and learning.

Stimming

Safe repetitive movement or sensory behaviour can support regulation. Focus on access and safety, not appearance.

Individualise: autistic learners are not one profile. Ask the student and whānau what helps, then monitor whether the support actually improves participation.
