Design the environment, not the student

Autistic learners vary widely. Useful support often focuses on communication clarity, predictability, sensory access, processing time and reducing unnecessary social or executive load.

Predictability

Preview what will happen

Show the sequence, who will be there, what will change and what stays the same.

Sensory

Notice invisible barriers

Noise, light, smell, touch and crowded movement can consume the capacity needed for learning.

Communication

Use precise language

Reduce ambiguity, allow processing time, and do not equate eye contact with listening.

Social demand

Structure group work

Assign roles, clarify expectations, and provide lower-social-demand alternatives when appropriate.

Interests

Use interests as bridges

Focused interests can support connection, motivation and depth of learning.

Stimming

Do not remove safe regulation

Safe repetitive movement can support attention and regulation. Focus on safety and access, not looking typical.

Try this instead

Instead of

“Look at me when I’m talking.”

Eye contact can increase processing load and is not a reliable measure of attention.

Try

“I’m going to give you two instructions. They’re also on the board.”

Measure understanding through the task rather than eye contact.
